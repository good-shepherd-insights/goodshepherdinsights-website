# Good Shepherd Insights Content and Query Audit

**Audit date:** 2026-08-30
**Site reviewed:** <https://goodshepherdinsights.com/>
**Input:** Current Google Search generative AI feature query report supplied by the site owner, plus a read-only live-site crawl and extraction using Tavily CLI.

## Executive finding

Good Shepherd Insights is a church-focused technology advisory firm. Its clearest offer is independent Fractional CTO leadership for mid-sized churches that need help choosing, securing, integrating, and governing technology.

The current query set is directionally aligned with that offer, especially around ChMS selection, church technology, data, automation, vendor evaluation, and cybersecurity. However, the queries show that Google is interpreting the site more broadly than the intended category. The site can be mistaken for a software vendor, dashboard provider, automation platform, managed IT provider, or general church research resource.

The central content problem is therefore not a lack of topics. It is a lack of hierarchy:

> Good Shepherd Insights is the independent technology leadership layer coordinating a church's software, data, security, vendors, and operations.

## What the site currently offers

The live site presents six service areas:

1. [ChMS Advisory](https://goodshepherdinsights.com/services/chms-advisory/) — selection, migration, integration, optimization, training, hosting, and infrastructure security.
2. [Church Cybersecurity & Risk Management](https://goodshepherdinsights.com/services/church-cybersecurity-risk-management/) — assessments, policies, security awareness, email security, and governance.
3. [Church Technology Strategy & Roadmapping](https://goodshepherdinsights.com/services/church-technology-strategy-roadmapping/) — a prioritized technology plan aligned with ministry goals, budget, and operational needs.
4. [Church Data Integration & Management](https://goodshepherdinsights.com/services/church-data-integration-management/) — connecting ChMS, giving, accounting, communications, websites, and other systems.
5. [Operations & Process Optimization](https://goodshepherdinsights.com/services/operations-process-optimization/) — workflow redesign and automation to reduce administrative overload.
6. [Church Vendor & Contract Management](https://goodshepherdinsights.com/services/church-vendor-contract-management/) — independent oversight of MSPs, SaaS licenses, AV providers, renewals, pricing, and vendor performance.

The umbrella model is the [Fractional CTO partnership](https://goodshepherdinsights.com/services/fractional-cto/). The repeated entry-point CTA is a Technology Strategy Audit.

## Intended customer

The [FAQ](https://goodshepherdinsights.com/faq/) states that GSI focuses on mid-sized churches with approximately 200–2,000 attendees. The site also describes an East Coast, USA focus.

The likely buying group is an executive pastor, senior pastor, operations or administrative director, finance director, board, IT employee, or IT volunteer responsible for technology decisions.

The strongest-fit customer is already experiencing one or more of these conditions:

- Too many disconnected software systems
- An underused or poorly configured ChMS
- Data mismatches between giving, accounting, communications, and membership systems
- Vendor pressure, confusing renewals, or suspected overpayment
- Cybersecurity, cyber-insurance, or business-continuity concerns
- Staff time lost to manual data entry and repetitive workflows
- A major software purchase, migration, or modernization decision without an internal technology leader

### Relevant, but currently under-explained

These queries are adjacent to the current offer, but the site does not make the expected deliverable sufficiently explicit:

- `church dashboards` / `church dashboard`
- `recurring giving analytics for churches`
- `ai automation for church admins`
- `church automation tools`
- `church automation systems`
- `church office automation`
- `church FAQ automation`
- `church cyber insurance`
- `church cyber liability insurance`
- `managed IT services for churches`
- `church managed it`
- `outsourced it for churches`
- `msp for churches`

These searches create two possible misconceptions:

1. A person may expect GSI to sell a dashboard, automation tool, or managed IT plan.
2. A person may not understand the difference between an independent Fractional CTO, an MSP, a cybersecurity assessor, and a ChMS vendor.

The site needs explicit pages explaining what GSI does, what it does not do, and how it coordinates with existing providers.

### Product- or terminology-led queries

The report includes:

- `shepherd's staff software`
- `shepherd staff software`
- `kristapp chms`
- `mms for churches`
- `mms for congregations`

These do not necessarily indicate demand for GSI. They may result from software names or from users using membership-management terminology instead of ChMS. The site should define the relationship between ChMS, church CRM, MMS, and ministry-management software rather than allowing the terms to remain ambiguous.

The Shepherd's Staff queries may also be a brand collision with “Good Shepherd.” Unless GSI deliberately serves that product's users, its content should not imply that it sells or supports Shepherd's Staff.

### Weakly aligned or likely noise

These queries do not represent the primary commercial audience:

- `quality of a good shepherd`
- `good insights`
- `good insight`
- `pastor framework`
- `pastor burnout statistics 2025 2026`
- `megachurch altar call alternatives digital decision cards case studies`
- `church codes`
- `give me sources`
- `conclusion and future scope of a church management software`

They have very low impression counts in the supplied report and should not drive the editorial strategy. They are likely semantic expansion, research behavior, brand ambiguity, or accidental matches.

### Geographic ambiguity

The report includes `business data insights consulting massachusetts` and `cybersecurity for churches north shore ma`. The live site communicates “East Coast, USA,” but does not clearly establish Massachusetts or North Shore Massachusetts as a primary service area. If Massachusetts is a target market, it needs an explicit service-area page. If it is not, these queries should be treated as noise rather than as a new positioning direction.

## The missing commercial connection

The query set rarely includes “Fractional CTO,” “technology strategy audit,” or “church technology consultant.” This suggests that Google understands the problems GSI discusses but does not consistently connect them to the company's flagship category.

Every problem page and educational article should make this relationship explicit:

`answer the question → identify the operational risk → explain GSI's independent role → offer the Technology Strategy Audit`

The audit should be described as a concrete deliverable, not only as a repeated CTA. A buyer should understand that it reviews the current software and vendor landscape, ChMS usage and data quality, integrations, security and continuity, workflows, automation opportunities, and technology spending, then produces a current-state map, prioritized 90-day actions, and a 12-month roadmap.

## Recommended content architecture

### Category pages

- Fractional CTO for Churches
- Church Technology Consulting
- Church Technology Strategy Audit
- Fractional CTO vs MSP vs IT Consultant

### Problem and service pages

- Church ChMS Selection and Migration
- Church Staff Workflow Automation
- Church Data Integration, Dashboards, and Analytics
- Church Cybersecurity and Cyber-Insurance Readiness
- Church Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery
- Church Vendor and Contract Management

### Decision-support content

- ChMS vs Church CRM vs MMS
- How to Compare Church Software Vendors
- Church Software Vendor Lock-In and Data Portability
- What a Church Technology Audit Includes
- When a Church Needs a Fractional CTO
- How to Build a Church Technology Roadmap

Each page should state whether GSI is advising, auditing, implementing, governing, or providing ongoing leadership. That distinction prevents the site from being mistaken for a platform vendor.

## Crawl and content observations

The live site returned the following crawl signals during the audit:

- `robots.txt` allows all user agents and points to the sitemap index.
- The sitemap contains the core commercial pages, legal pages, FAQ, and blog content.
- The homepage exposes aligned title, description, canonical, robots, Open Graph, Twitter, and JSON-LD metadata.
- Tavily mapped several URLs that returned 404 content, including `/consultation`, `/pillar/ai-in-church-management-software`, `/ai-church-management-software-guide`, and `/ai-in-church-management-software-2026`. These URLs were not present in the current sitemap and should be checked for stale internal links or historical discovery.
- The Contact page uses “Request a Security Review,” while the dominant site-wide CTA is “Get a Technology Strategy Audit.” That creates two competing first-step offers.
- Commercial pages repeat statistics and differentiator claims without consistently showing the supporting source beside each claim. Prominent claims should be sourced or qualified.

## Success definition

The site should cause both a crawler and a prospective customer to reach the same conclusion:

> Good Shepherd Insights does not sell a ChMS, dashboard, automation platform, or managed IT plan. It provides independent technology leadership that helps a mid-sized church evaluate, govern, integrate, secure, and improve those systems.

The strongest measurement is not simply more impressions. It is increased visibility for qualified advisory queries, clearer landing-page alignment, and movement from problem-oriented searches to the Technology Strategy Audit and Fractional CTO pages.
