# Good Shepherd Insights Competitor Positioning Benchmark

**Benchmark date:** 2026-08-30
**Purpose:** Identify organizations that correctly communicate church technology advisory, ChMS consulting, Fractional CTO, and managed-IT boundaries.

## Scope and classification

The correct competitive set is not every church technology company. It should be separated into:

1. **Direct advisory competitors** — independent or strategic church-technology advisors.
2. **Adjacent service competitors** — managed IT, cybersecurity, or data firms that may compete for part of the engagement.
3. **Positioning benchmarks** — Fractional CTO firms with a useful packaging model but a different audience.
4. **Software vendors** — ChMS, giving, engagement, and church-platform companies. These are search competitors and content references, but not independent advisory competitors.

## Closest competitors and benchmarks

### Enable Ministry Partners — closest overall competitor

- [Strategic Consulting Solutions](https://enableministry.com/what-we-do/strategic-consulting-solutions)
- [Church Management Software Consulting](https://enableministry.com/church-specific-support/church-management-software-consulting)

Enable combines church-specific technology strategy with CIO planning, long-range roadmaps, environment assessments, productivity-tool integration, ChMS selection, implementation, and vendor contract management.

What Enable does well:

- Leads with strategic consulting for churches rather than a product category.
- Connects technology decisions to ministry mission and stewardship.
- Uses the buyer's language: select, optimize, implement, integrate, and use data.
- Publishes supporting resources around the same service themes.
- Links educational content to a clear consulting conversation.

Lesson for GSI: make Fractional CTO leadership the umbrella category and frame ChMS, security, data, vendors, and operations as components of that leadership relationship.

### 5K Technical Services — strongest service-packaging benchmark

- [Church Management Software consulting](https://www.5ktech.com/services/church-management-software)

5K packages its church software consulting into three levels:

- **Connect:** clarify needs, create a basic specification, and identify possible software.
- **Select:** support evaluation and selection, then develop the implementation plan.
- **Serve:** manage needs analysis, selection, implementation, testing, training, and service planning.

5K also explicitly states that it does not resell, distribute, or receive royalties from software providers. Its page describes requirements interviews, custom RFPs, scoring, demonstrations, peer references, implementation, testing, and training. It includes testimonials tied to named church roles.

What 5K does well:

- Makes the first engagement understandable.
- Gives the buyer a visible progression from discovery to execution.
- Describes actual deliverables rather than only benefits.
- States independence in plain language.
- Uses church-specific proof.

Lesson for GSI: productize the Technology Strategy Audit and show its phases, deliverables, timeline, and optional continuation into Fractional CTO leadership.

### Rooted Software — managed-IT boundary benchmark

- [Church IT Support and Managed IT Services](https://rooted.software/church-it-support)

Rooted is an adjacent managed IT provider, not a direct Fractional CTO competitor. Its page clearly explains included services such as help desk, monitoring, endpoint security, patching, device management, network support, and optional CIO consultation. It also publishes indicative per-user pricing and a discovery-call path.

What Rooted does well:

- States exactly what managed IT includes.
- Separates recurring operational support from strategic CIO consulting.
- Gives the buyer a way to estimate cost.
- Explains who the service is for.

Lesson for GSI: publish a clear “Fractional CTO vs MSP” explanation. State that GSI can guide, govern, and manage the relationship with an MSP without automatically replacing the MSP.

### Kingdom CTO — strongest Fractional CTO packaging benchmark

- [Kingdom CTO Services](https://kingdomcto.com)

Kingdom CTO serves Christian digital businesses rather than churches, so it is a positioning benchmark rather than a direct church competitor. Its homepage presents a named CTO, a three-phase approach, client proof, a founder story, and three engagement options:

- Ongoing Fractional CTO leadership
- A defined strategy and vision engagement
- Lightweight advisory support

The page includes example time commitments and typical investments.

What Kingdom CTO does well:

- Defines Fractional CTO in plain language.
- Shows a sequence from foundation to team enablement to strategic direction.
- Makes the person and accountability visible.
- Offers distinct engagement models.
- Uses testimonials and client references.

Lesson for GSI: explain whether the customer is buying an audit, a defined roadmap engagement, ongoing executive participation, or a light advisory relationship.

### Enable's roadmap content — strongest topic-to-offer bridge

- [Strategic IT Roadmap for Churches](https://enableministry.com/resources/strategic-it-roadmap-for-churches-why-it-matters-and-how-to-start)

This resource starts with the church's operational reality, explains why reactive technology decisions create waste and risk, and ends with a strategy consultation CTA. It is a useful model for turning informational searches into qualified advisory conversations.

Lesson for GSI: articles targeting “church dashboards,” “church automation,” “church business continuity,” and “church vendor questions” should each end with a concrete explanation of how those issues are evaluated in the Technology Strategy Audit.

## Adjacent competitors to distinguish, not imitate

### Managed IT and security firms

Examples surfaced during the benchmark include:

- [Infinity Technology Consulting — IT Support for Churches](https://infinitytechconsulting.com/industries-we-serve/churches)
- [Lamb Telecom — Church Cybersecurity](https://lambtele.com/blog/church-cybersecurity-essential-policies-for-protection)

These firms compete on support, networks, monitoring, infrastructure, and security operations. GSI should not copy their primary offer unless managed services are genuinely part of its business. Instead, it should explain how independent technology leadership coordinates with those providers.

### Church data and engagement platforms

Examples include [StudioC](https://thestudioc.org) and [TouchPoint](https://www.touchpointsoftware.com/about-us). These companies sell or operate technology platforms, dashboards, engagement systems, or integrated ChMS capabilities. Their content is useful for understanding customer vocabulary, but their commercial incentives differ from GSI's claimed vendor-neutral role.

### ChMS and giving vendors

Pushpay, Subsplash, ACS Technologies, Tithely, and similar companies should be treated as platform vendors, not independent advisory competitors. Their content naturally leads toward selecting or using their own technology.

## Recommended GSI offer architecture

### Category statement

> Independent Fractional CTO leadership for mid-sized churches.

### Customer problem

> Your church has too many systems, unclear vendor decisions, security exposure, unreliable data, or no technology owner.

### Entry offer

> Technology Strategy Audit.

### Audit deliverable

The audit should visibly include:

- Current-state software and vendor map
- ChMS usage and data-quality review
- Integration and reporting review
- Cybersecurity and business-continuity review
- Staff workflow and automation opportunities
- Vendor, contract, and technology-spend findings
- Prioritized 90-day action plan
- 12-month technology roadmap
- Board-ready summary

### Ongoing offer

> Fractional CTO partnership to govern vendors, security, data, systems, and implementation.

### Boundary statement

> GSI does not sell a preferred ChMS, dashboard, automation platform, or managed IT package. It helps the church evaluate, govern, integrate, secure, and improve the systems it chooses.

## Content pages to build from the benchmark

1. **Fractional CTO for Churches** — define the role and distinguish it from a consultant, MSP, IT director, and vendor.
2. **Technology Strategy Audit** — explain scope, deliverables, timeline, and next steps.
3. **Church ChMS Selection and Migration** — use 5K-style process detail while preserving GSI's independent position.
4. **Church Data Integration, Dashboards, and Analytics** — explain whether GSI advises, designs, implements, or governs each layer.
5. **Church Automation and Workflow Consulting** — answer the staff-automation query cluster without implying GSI sells automation software.
6. **Church Cybersecurity, Managed IT, and Insurance Readiness** — explain the boundaries between assessment, MSP support, cyber insurance, and incident response.
7. **ChMS vs CRM vs MMS** — resolve terminology-driven searches.
8. **Vendor Lock-In and Data Portability** — address contract, export, integration, and renewal risk.

## What GSI should not copy

GSI should not position itself as a ChMS vendor, giving platform, church app, dashboard product, or generic managed IT provider. Those companies may rank for valuable searches, but copying their sales language would undermine the site's most defensible differentiator: independent leadership on the church's side of the table.

## Benchmark conclusion

GSI already has the service inventory needed to compete. The gap is commercial structure and category clarity.

The strongest combined model is:

- Enable's strategic consulting architecture
- 5K's vendor-neutral, tiered ChMS engagement
- Rooted's managed-IT versus CIO boundary
- Kingdom CTO's Fractional CTO packaging and proof

The resulting customer journey should be:

`technology confusion → Technology Strategy Audit → roadmap → project oversight → ongoing Fractional CTO leadership`
