# Key Service Page Components

This is a generic, scalable template. It defines what each component should
contain and why it exists. It does not hardcode service-specific claims, copy,
audiences, metrics, vendors, pricing, or outcomes.

## 1. Clear Buyer/Problem Hero

### Content

- Service name.
- Intended buyer or buyer role.
- The problem or decision the service addresses.
- The primary outcome in buyer language.
- One primary call to action.

### Why

The visitor should understand what the service is, whether it applies to them,
and what happens next without relying on the rest of the site.

## 2. Audience and Fit Qualification

### Content

- Situations that indicate a good fit.
- Buyer roles involved in the decision.
- Relevant organizational, operational, or technical context.
- Explicit non-fit conditions where appropriate.

### Why

Qualification reduces ambiguity, improves lead quality, and helps the visitor
decide whether to continue.

## 3. Defined Engagement Process

### Content

- Ordered phases of the engagement.
- What happens in each phase.
- Inputs required from the client.
- Outputs produced by the service provider.
- Decision points, dependencies, and timing guidance when verified.

### Why

Professional services are purchased under uncertainty. A visible process makes
the work, responsibilities, and commitment understandable.

## 4. Service-Specific Outcomes

### Content

- The practical changes the service is intended to produce.
- Outcomes tied to the service, not generic company benefits.
- Baselines, measurement methods, or evidence when available.
- Clear distinction between expected outcomes and guarantees.

### Why

Buyers need to understand the value of the engagement and how success will be
recognized.

## 5. Deliverable and Artifact Previews

### Content

- The tangible deliverables produced.
- A representative outline, screenshot, sample, or description where allowed.
- The decisions or actions each deliverable supports.
- Confidentiality or availability limits where applicable.

### Why

Concrete artifacts make an intangible service easier to evaluate before contact.

## 6. Case-Study Proof

### Content

- Client context, with permission or anonymization status.
- Starting problem.
- Work performed.
- Result or current status.
- Relevant timeframe and measurement basis.
- Link to the full case study when available.

### Why

Case studies demonstrate that the provider has handled a comparable problem;
they should connect directly to the service claims they support.

## 7. Named Expertise and Credentials

### Content

- People responsible for delivering the service.
- Relevant experience, qualifications, certifications, or domain expertise.
- Role in the engagement.
- Links to fuller biographies or professional evidence where appropriate.

### Why

The buyer is evaluating the people and judgment behind the service, not only a
capability list.

## 8. Client Testimonials and Logos

### Content

- Client quote tied to the specific service or outcome.
- Client name, role, and organization when permission exists.
- Relevant client logos with usage permission.
- Context that prevents a quote from being detached from its evidence.

### Why

Relevant third-party evidence reduces perceived risk and supports credibility.

## 9. Risk and Governance Information

### Content

- Risks the engagement addresses.
- Data, security, privacy, or continuity considerations relevant to the service.
- Decision ownership and responsibilities across client, provider, and vendors.
- Constraints, exclusions, and escalation paths.

### Why

Executive and operational buyers need to understand how risk is controlled and
who is accountable for decisions.

## 10. Service-Specific FAQs

### Content

- Questions about fit and timing.
- Questions about scope, inputs, and deliverables.
- Questions about platforms, dependencies, or implementation responsibility.
- Questions about evidence, confidentiality, and next steps.

### Why

FAQs address objections and information gaps that can prevent a qualified buyer
from taking the next step.

## 11. Investment and Engagement Options

### Content

- Engagement models or starting points.
- What determines scope, duration, and cost.
- Included and excluded work.
- Pricing only when verified and approved for publication.
- A way to request a scoped estimate when pricing cannot be published.

### Why

Buyers need enough commercial context to determine whether a conversation is
worth pursuing.

## 12. Secondary Research Paths

### Content

- Relevant guides, assessments, research, or explainers.
- Related case studies or proof.
- A clear indication of who each resource is for.
- A useful next step for visitors who are not ready to contact the provider.

### Why

High-consideration buyers often research before engaging. A secondary path
supports that decision without forcing an immediate sales action.

## 13. Contextual Related-Service Links

### Content

- Adjacent services that logically follow from the visitor's current problem.
- A short explanation of the relationship between services.
- Links to the relevant service pages.
- No unrelated service inventory.

### Why

Contextual navigation helps visitors continue their evaluation without losing
the original problem or service context.

## Template usage rules

- Keep the component structure reusable across services.
- Populate fields from the service's verified source of truth.
- Do not invent claims, metrics, testimonials, credentials, pricing, or outcomes.
- Do not add a component merely because a competitor uses it; require a verified
  buyer or business need.
- Keep service-specific content in content data, not in the reusable component
  implementation.
